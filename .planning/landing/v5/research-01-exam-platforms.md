# Research: How Assessment / Exam / Coding-Test Platforms Structure Their Marketing Pages

Fetched 2026-10-19. Research for the Pariksha (free, open-source, self-hosted exam platform) landing-page rewrite.

---

## 1. What the category does (one page)

### The modal section spine

Across every site in this research — hiring-tech (HackerRank, HackerEarth, Codility, CoderPad, Mercer Mettl, Testlify, Xobin, Talview), exam/quiz tools (ClassMarker, Testportal, SpeedExam, Testmoz), proctoring vendors (Proctorio, Talview, Eklavvya), and LMS/academic platforms (Canvas, Moodle) — the same skeleton repeats, with depth scaling to how enterprise the buyer is:

1. **Hero**: H1 (increasingly an AI-era repositioning line, not a feature line) + one-line subhead + primary CTA ("Book/Request a Demo" dominant) + secondary CTA (varies: free trial, "explore solutions," product-specific link).
2. **Logo wall**: 6–16 recognizable enterprise logos, immediately under the hero, no caption needed.
3. **Value props (3–5 up)**: short cards, one per buyer pain (speed, fairness, integrity, scale).
4. **Product tour**: tabbed or stacked sections per product line (e.g. HackerEarth's Source/Screen/Interview/Engage/Upskill; Codility's Screen/Interview/Skills Intelligence; CoderPad's Qualify/Screen/Interview).
5. **Use-case tabs**: by persona or segment (education vs corporate; volume hiring vs technical hiring; K-12 vs higher-ed vs workforce).
6. **Proof/stats band**: a dense strip of hard numbers (candidates assessed, uptime %, countries, customers).
7. **Security/compliance band**: logo-style badges (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, CCPA, WCAG) — almost always present, rarely explained.
8. **Case studies / testimonials carousel**: named company + named metric + a quote, sometimes a full case-study page link.
9. **Integrations band**: ATS/HRIS/calendar/LMS logos.
10. **Pricing teaser or omission**: most B2B players hide pricing entirely behind "Talk to us"/demo; only prosumer/SMB tools (Testportal, ClassMarker, SpeedExam, Testmoz, TestGorilla) show a free tier or self-serve signup above the fold.
11. **FAQ** (longer-tail enterprise sites only; iMocha runs 20 Q&As).
12. **Final CTA repeat** — same CTA as the hero, restated.
13. **Footer**: product/solutions/resources/company/legal, plus trust badges repeated.

### The 5 most-copied moves

1. **"Talk to us" / "Book a demo" as the only real path to purchase**, even when a free trial exists — pricing is hidden almost everywhere except the prosumer tier of the category (Testportal, ClassMarker, SpeedExam, TestGorilla, Testmoz all show free/self-serve; Codility, CoderPad, HackerEarth, iMocha, Talview, Questionmark do not show price at all).
2. **A number-dense proof strip right after the logo wall** — "2.5M+ users," "20,000 engineering teams," "300M+ exams proctored," "99.99% uptime." Every single site in this set uses at least one hard number within the first two screens.
3. **Named customer + named metric, not generic testimonial** — "HCL Technologies: 80% reduction in proxy attempts," "PwC: 54% decrease in time to hire," "Unity: saved 2,200 hours." Vague praise quotes are being phased out in favor of attributed, numeric outcomes.
4. **Security/compliance as a logo row, not an argument** — SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / GDPR / CCPA badges are dropped in as a visual trust signal with almost no accompanying prose explaining what they protect against. Only the proctoring specialists (Proctorio, Talview) actually narrate the privacy/control story in sentences.
5. **2026 repositioning around AI, on both sides of the product** — nearly every site now opens with an AI-era line, either "AI changed what we test for" (Codility: "AI can write code now. Hire the engineers who can build"; CoderPad: "Every role is technical now. Hire like it.") or "AI threatens exam integrity, we stop it" (Talview: "ENTERPRISE-READY AI PROCTORING AND AI INTERVIEWER"; ExamSoft: "Secure assessment in the age of AI"; Testlify's AI interviewer pitch). This is the single most consistent 2026 shift versus older cached copies of these same pages.

## 3. Per-site detail (section spine, proof devices, category anxieties, pricing, audience, notable)

### HackerRank for Work
- **Retrieval note**: `/business/` and `/business` both 404'd; `/recruit/` served a 404 (branded, with an xkcd comic easter egg — itself a small brand-voice data point: even their error page is engineer-coded humor, consistent with "written for engineers" critique that also applies to our own current page).
- Everything else here is **NOT RETRIEVED** and omitted rather than guessed, per instructions.

### HackerEarth (Recruit)
- **Section spine**: Hero w/ product screenshot → logo wall (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Trimble, IBM, GlobalLogic, Barclays, PayPal, Intuit) → value-prop block → five-pillar tabbed product tour (Source / Screen / Interview / Engage / Upskill) → enterprise-readiness/certifications → integrations statement → support-capability callout (95% CSAT, 1-hr turnaround, "0 bots") → named case studies (Amazon, Microsoft, GlobalLogic, Trimble, Siemens) → blog carousel → final CTA → footer.
- **Proof devices**: 10 enterprise logos; numeric stats (10M+ developers, 25K+ assessment questions, 1,000+ skills, 40+ languages, 40,000+ problems, 99.99% uptime, 95% CSAT, 3,000+ companies, 9.6M+ developer community); named case-study quotes attributed to specific companies (e.g. Amazon: "We can assess 1000+ candidates simultaneously using automated skill evaluation").
- **Category anxieties**: Scale/reliability is handled hard with "99.99% server uptime guarantee" and "assess 1000+ candidates simultaneously." Cheating/integrity is addressed only lightly in blog-tier content ("identity verification tied to the interview session"), not foregrounded on the homepage. Compliance badges shown: ISO 27001:2013, ISO 27017:2018, EEOC, OFCCP — notably US employment-law badges (EEOC/OFCCP), not just infosec ones, because the buyer is HR/Talent Acquisition in regulated hiring.
- **Pricing**: none on page; demo-gated.
- **Written for**: Recruiter + engineering manager jointly — vocabulary mixes "tech hiring," "developer community," and hard SLA numbers (uptime, CSAT, turnaround time) that read as procurement reassurance.
- **Notable**: Good — the "0 bots" support stat is a sharp, specific trust signal (implies humans answer support, not chatbots) that is more memorable than the standard "24/7 support."

### Codility
- **Section spine**: Hero → logo wall (GitHub, SpaceX, Tesla, EY, LSEG, SAP, Barclays, Citi) → 3-part value prop (Screen/Interview/Skills Intelligence) → AI customization → VS Code assessment feature → skills-based-hiring stats → platform capability deep dive → testimonials → detailed customer stories → candidate-experience stories → compliance/security → final CTA → footer.
- **Proof devices**: G2 rating 4.6 w/ Leader badges (Enterprise & Mid-Market); "20,000 engineering teams"; Unity case study ("750 candidate tests over 90 days, saving 2,200 hours of interview time"); industry stats (81% skills-based hiring, 94% skills predict success, 70% employers shifting, 90% fewer mis-hires).
- **Category anxieties**: Compliance band is explicit and labeled: "SOC 2 audited," "ISO 27001 certified," "CCPA and GDPR compliant," "WCAG 2.2 AA accessible" — notably pairs accessibility compliance with data compliance, unusual breadth. Anti-cheat: "identity verification, impersonation detection, proctoring mentioned" but not foregrounded in hero.
- **Pricing**: "Request a free trial" exists as secondary CTA, but no price shown; "Talk to us" is primary.
- **Written for**: VP Eng / Talent Acquisition leadership at large enterprises — vocabulary is "skills intelligence," "signal," G2 Leader badge for social proof aimed at procurement-adjacent buyers doing vendor comparison.
- **Notable**: Good — leads with a blunt, current anxiety ("AI can write code now") rather than a generic feature headline; this is the sharpest, most buyer-relevant hero line found in the whole set.

### CoderPad
- **Section spine**: Hero → "Trusted by 4,000+ customers worldwide" trust line → 4-up value props (developer workflow, completion rates, skill testing, cheating detection) → product tour (Qualify/Screen/Interview) → security/compliance → integrations → 13-logo customer carousel → AI-advisor prompt section.
- **Proof devices**: "96% of candidates finish our assessments," "97% of engineers prefer it," "60% higher completion than other platforms," "Save 33+ hours per hire," SOC-2 Type 2 (Insight Assurance certified); named customers Box, ALTEN, Gameloft, monday.com, McAfee, Cresta.
- **Category anxieties**: Cheating/integrity is one of only 4 headline value props ("cheating detection") — unusually prominent placement vs. peers who bury it. Scale: "4,000+ customers" and completion-rate stats double as a reliability signal (candidates don't drop off mid-test).
- **Pricing**: Not shown on homepage.
- **Written for**: Engineering hiring managers — the rebranded H1 ("Every role is technical now. Hire like it.") is explicitly trying to widen the buyer from "dev hiring" to "any hiring," which is a notable 2026 strategic pivot.
- **Notable**: Good — candidate-completion-rate stats (96%, 97%, 60% higher) are a proof device almost nobody else uses, and it directly answers "will candidates actually finish this," a real anxiety for recruiters picking a tool.

### TestGorilla

- **Category anxieties**: Not substantially visible in the retrieved portion.
- **Pricing**: "Try for free" present in nav, implying a self-serve/freemium entry point alongside demo path — dual-motion GTM (PLG + sales-assist), unusual among the enterprise-leaning set.

- **Notable**: Good — "Find great talent. Prove it." is a 5-word subhead that does more positioning work than most 2-sentence subheads elsewhere; worth studying as a brevity model.

### Mercer | Mettl
- **Section spine**: Hero → logo wall (Marsh/Mercer branding) → 3-pillar value props → product tour → use-case tabs (customer stories) → proof/stats → integrations → security/compliance → insights/resources teaser → final CTA → footer.
- **Proof devices**: Dense stat block — "6,000+ organizations," "700+ educational institutions," "100+ countries," "25+ industries," "100k+ questions," "25+ languages," "25M assessments conducted annually," "95% accuracy in cheating detection" (IIMB case study); named case studies: Tata Play Fiber, Masai, IIMB, CHRIST University, ISB, Coforge.
- **Category anxieties**: Most thorough anti-cheat language in the set — "AI and human-based proctoring," "Advanced anti-cheating controls," "Multi-layered proctoring system," "95% accuracy in cheating detection," all attributed to a named institutional case study (IIMB) rather than asserted abstractly. Compliance badges unusually deep for the category: ISO 27001, ISO 9001-2015, GDPR, TÜV, NABCB, APA, SIOP, US Seal — includes psychometric body certifications (APA/SIOP), not just infosec, because Mettl sells scientifically-validated assessments to HR/L&D as much as to recruiters.
- **Pricing**: None; demo-gated.
- **Written for**: Mix of corporate L&D/HR and higher-ed admissions — the inclusion of "700+ educational institutions" alongside enterprise logos (Accenture, Adobe, Amazon, Microsoft) signals a dual ICP, directly relevant to Pariksha's own dual buyer (placement cells + companies).
- **Notable**: Good — attaching a hard cheating-detection accuracy number (95%) to one named institution is the single best model in this research for how to make an anti-cheat claim feel earned rather than marketing fluff.

### Testlify
- **Section spine**: Hero → logo wall ("1,500+" clients) → "resume-first vs skills-first" comparison → 4-step how-it-works → test-library showcase → skills-intelligence/scoring demo → platform tour (assessments/interviews/resume screening) → use-case tabs (volume hiring, remote, campus) → testimonials → global-reach messaging → research reports → integrations → security/compliance → FAQ → final CTA → footer.
- **Proof devices**: "1,500+ talent teams," "3,500+ validated tests," "94% candidate satisfaction," "55% faster hiring," "Screen a full pipeline in 30 minutes, not days," "up to 75% less screening time."
- **Category anxieties**: Anti-cheat framed candidate-side as fairness, not just enforcement: "Non-googleable questions with advanced proctoring," "fair, proctored experience." Compliance band broad: SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, EEOC, EFPA (European psychometric standards body), CCPA, GDPR.
- **Pricing**: "Try for free" is the primary CTA — 7-day free trial, no credit card required, stated explicitly. One of the few enterprise-grade tools leading with a real self-serve offer.
- **Written for**: HR/TA at mid-market companies, with "campus hiring" called out as a distinct use case — directly adjacent to Pariksha's placement-cell buyer.
- **Notable**: Good — "campus hiring" as a named use-case tab is a direct analog to Pariksha's college-placement-cell ICP; worth mirroring language, not just structure.

### ClassMarker
- **Section spine**: Hero → logo wall (Google, Yahoo, Microsoft, LinkedIn, Facebook, Amazon, Netflix — as *users* of the tool, not necessarily paying enterprise accounts) → 5-feature value grid (Security, Easy creation, Control, Question Banks, Results) → 4-step product tour (Create → Setup → Give exam → Analyze/Certify) → feature deep-dives (security, time-saving, proctoring, affordability) → API/integrations → use cases (recruitment, mock exams, surveys) → PDF lead magnet → final CTA → footer with G2 badge.
- **Proof devices**: "over 99% uptime and 24/7 monitoring"; G2 4.4/5; "Celebrating 20 years" (est. 2006) as a longevity/trust signal.
- **Category anxieties**: Proctoring described plainly: tab-switching detection, mouse-boundary restriction, camera monitoring, face detection, ID-card verification — functional, not emotionally reassuring language. Compliance: GDPR and CCPA only (lighter than enterprise peers, consistent with SMB positioning).
- **Pricing**: No number on homepage, but explicitly names the pricing *model*: "Subscription plans" + "pay-as-you-go Credit Packs" + "discounts for educators and non-profits" — a rare instance of a vendor being concrete about *how* pricing works even without showing a number.
- **Written for**: Small business / training / educator admin — plain, non-jargon language ("Saving you hours of paperwork"), oldest brand voice in the set (est. 2006, and it reads that way — functional, not polished).
- **Notable**: Good — explicitly naming "discounts for educators and non-profits" is a concrete, low-cost trust signal that a free/open-source tool like Pariksha could out-do trivially (we are free, full stop) if we make the comparison implicit.

### iMocha
- **Section spine**: Hero → logo wall (L'Oréal, Ericsson, Deloitte, EY, FPT, Philip Morris) → "Look Beyond the Label" value prop → 3 industry comparison case studies → analyst endorsements (Everest Group, IDC, Brandon Hall) → explanatory section on "Two Intelligences" → 5 use-case tabs → integration partners → unified-system pitch → 4 detailed case studies → AI agents registry → "Why iMocha" list → 20-question FAQ → final CTA → footer.
- **Proof devices**: Analyst badges are the standout device here — Everest Group PEAK MATRIX ("Major Contender & Star Performer"), IDC MarketScape ("Major Player"), Brandon Hall Gold — nobody else in this set leans on third-party analyst recognition this hard. "300+ enterprises," "10,000+ skill evaluations."
- **Category anxieties**: Proctoring mentioned only in passing, no detail. Compliance: SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001:2022, GDPR.
- **Pricing**: None; demo/expert-call gated.
- **Written for**: Chief People Officer / HR analytics buyer — "context layer," "work intelligence," "defensible view" is boardroom/analyst vocabulary, not recruiter-operational language. This is the most "enterprise platform" voice in the set, arguably over-engineered for a mid-market buyer.
- **Notable**: Bad — the H1 ("The Context Layer for Skills & Work Intelligence") is abstract enough that a first-time visitor cannot tell this is a skills-assessment company; buries the actual product category. A caution case for us: don't abstract the headline away from "online exam platform."

### Xobin
- **Section spine**: Hero → featured research callout → logo wall ("5,000+ customers") → skills framework (Functional/AI Fluency/Behavioral) → research deep-dive → testimonial → Gartner badge → AI Interviews section → platform capability grid (9 items) → Claude AI integration showcase → "Xobin Grow" intro → use-case tabs ("Who uses Xobin") → integrations/security → testimonials carousel → news → demo form → footer.
- **Proof devices**: "5,305,250 assessments conducted" (precise, not rounded — a deliberate credibility device), "5,000+ customers," "55+ countries," "4.8/5 G2," Gartner recognition badge.
- **Category anxieties**: Anti-cheat stated plainly: "Identity checks, browser controls and AI monitoring keep remote assessments honest, so scores from anywhere stay trustworthy enough to act on" — ties proctoring directly to score trustworthiness, a good causal framing. Compliance: SOC 2 Type I & II, ISO 27001, GDPR, CSA STAR, ISO 42001 (AI management) — the ISO 42001 badge is new/rare and signals they're pre-empting AI-governance scrutiny.
- **Pricing**: None on homepage; links out to a pricing page.
- **Written for**: HR/TA with a secondary audience of CHRO (the "Human vs AI Skills Report" content-marketing hook) — mixes operational and thought-leadership content on one page.
- **Notable**: Good — the exact, non-rounded assessment count ("5,305,250") reads as more credible than a rounded "5M+" because it implies a live counter, not a marketing estimate.

### Proctorio
- **Section spine**: Hero → trust-metric callout → 3-pillar product tour (Integrity, Origin, Vault) → 4-column feature grid → 3-column value props (Human-centered AI / Purpose-built technology / Institutional control) → 4-column use-case tabs (K-12, Higher ed, Workforce, Credentialing) → 2-column proof/credibility (accessibility + compliance) → 3-CTA cluster → footer.
- **Proof devices**: "300M+ exams proctored globally"; "Since 2013, organizations worldwide have trusted Proctorio to protect millions of assessments."
- **Category anxieties — this is the central product, so it's the most instructive site for anxiety-handling**: Cheating credibility: "Ensuring integrity by verifying identities, securing testing environments, and proctoring sessions." Privacy/control handled as an *institutional control* promise, not a technology promise: "Institutions decide how AI is used. Proctorio provides configurable tools and insights, and every decision stays with the institution." — this directly answers "will this spy on / punish my students unfairly," by putting the institution (not the vendor) in control. Scale/reliability: the "300M+ exams" + "since 2013" combo is a tenure-plus-volume one-two punch.
- **Pricing**: None shown.
- **Written for**: University IT/registrar and academic-integrity office, not a recruiter — "institutional control," "configurable toggles" is governance language.
- **Notable**: Good — "every decision stays with the institution" is the single best phrase found anywhere in this research for defusing proctoring/privacy anxiety, because it reframes the vendor as a tool-provider, not a decision-maker. Directly reusable idea for Pariksha's self-hosted pitch ("your data never leaves your server" is the open-source-native version of the same promise, and arguably stronger).

### Talview
- **Section spine**: Hero (multi-CTA, no single primary) → logo wall → "Built to be embedded" value prop → 3 integration-mode diagram → cheating-prevalence problem statement → 4 core capabilities → 3-column product comparison (Identity/Alvy/Ivy) → human-in-loop framework → customer-achievement stats → cost/risk messaging → "Verity" identity-assurance block → G2 badges → use-case tabs ("Start where your pain is") → FAQ → final CTA ("Run one exam through it") → footer.
- **Proof devices**: Named stats per customer — "HCL Technologies: 80% reduction in proxy attempts," "PwC: 54% decrease in time to hire," "La Trobe University: 12,000 students per semester," "GBTS: scaled from 2,000 to 30,000 test-takers." Stat on AI-cheating prevalence itself used as a hook: "AI use in assessed higher education: 71% (2024) → 88% (2025)."
- **Category anxieties**: Explicitly engineers trust around automation by foregrounding "human-in-loop": "system flags, humans decide final outcome" — this is a deliberate answer to the fear that an algorithm alone expels/fails a student unfairly. Compliance: GDPR, WCAG 2.1A, SOC 2, ISO 27001. A specific patent is cited (US Patent 12,361,115 B1) as a credibility device for its detection tech — unique in this set.
- **Pricing**: Explicit teaser section labeled "What it costs you" exists but no number retrieved — framed as a cost-of-inaction section (cost of *not* buying), not a price list.
- **Written for**: Mixed — "embedded"/"SDK and API docs" CTA signals a developer/platform-integrator buyer alongside the usual HR/academic-integrity buyer; unusually technical for this category.
- **Notable**: Good — "human-in-loop, system flags, humans decide" is a second strong reusable phrase for defusing automation-anxiety, complementary to Proctorio's institutional-control framing.

- **Pricing**: "Sign up - it's free" is the primary CTA, explicit and above the fold — one of the clearest free-tier-forward hero CTAs in the set.
- **Written for**: Dual corporate-training and classroom-teacher buyer, skewing SMB — plain language ("Turn your assessments into success stories").
- **Notable**: Good — the "trees saved" stat is a clever, differentiated proof device: it's a byproduct scale metric that nobody can fake easily and that appeals to a values-driven buyer (relevant if Pariksha wants a "why open-source/self-hosted" values angle rather than only a cost angle).

## 4. Patterns worth stealing for an open-source, self-hosted, free product

1. **"Control stays with you" as the privacy answer, not a compliance badge.** Proctorio's "Institutions decide how AI is used... every decision stays with the institution" is the best-in-category phrase for defusing the privacy/control anxiety — and Pariksha's self-hosted architecture makes an even stronger, factually true version available: candidate data never leaves the buyer's own server/database, full stop, no vendor data-processing agreement needed. This beats every SOC 2/ISO badge in this research because it's structural, not promised.
2. **Lead the free/open claim with scale and seriousness, not apology.** Moodle's pattern (even seen only in secondary snippets) is to state "200,000-student University," "100,000+ sites," "140+ languages" before ever mentioning "free" or "open source" as a line item — free is folded into an identity claim of maturity and reach, not framed defensively. This directly contradicts the brief's description of the current Pariksha page (pre-alpha everywhere, 9-item "who this is NOT for" list) and is the single highest-leverage structural fix available.
3. **Named-customer-plus-hard-number testimonials, not quote blocks.** "HCL Technologies: 80% reduction in proxy attempts," "Unity: saved 2,200 hours," "IIMB: 95% cheating-detection accuracy." Pariksha should design its own proof devices this way from day one (even with early pilot users) rather than using unattributed praise.
4. **Tiered proctoring/rigor as a self-serve ladder (SpeedExam's ProctorLite / Audio&Video / AI-based model).** Mirrors well onto an open-source product: "run it yourself for free" → "add webcam proctoring" → "add AI flagging" — gives a buyer a way to self-qualify without a sales call, which matters because a free self-hosted tool likely won't have (or want) a heavy-handed sales-demo gate like the enterprise players.
5. **A values-driven proof device alongside the usual numbers (Testportal's "trees saved").** Open-source and self-hosted already imply a values story (data sovereignty, no vendor lock-in, no per-candidate fee bleeding a nonprofit placement cell dry); a concrete, quantifiable version of that story (e.g., "institutions have run N exams with zero per-candidate cost," or a running total of exams conducted across all self-hosted instances if that's ever instrumentable) would be a genuinely novel proof device in this category.
6. **Human-in-the-loop framing for any AI/automation claims (Talview's "system flags, humans decide").** If Pariksha ever ships proctoring or AI-assisted grading, borrow this phrasing rather than asserting the AI "catches cheaters."
7. **Campus/placement-cell as a named, explicit use-case tab (Testlify's "campus hiring").** Directly matches one of Pariksha's named buyer segments; don't bury this under a generic "education" tab.
8. **State the pricing *model* even without a number (ClassMarker's "subscription + pay-as-you-go credit packs," Eklavvya's "per-candidate / annual / enterprise license," SpeedExam's "no user fees, charged per exam attempt").** For Pariksha this is trivial and uniquely strong: the honest model is "$0, forever, self-hosted" — stating this plainly and specifically (not just "free tier available") removes the single biggest buyer hesitation in this entire category (hidden/escalating per-candidate costs).

## 5. Patterns to reject

1. **Hiding price behind "Talk to us" when the honest answer is "free."** This is the dominant category pattern (Codility, CoderPad, iMocha, Talview, Mercer Mettl, HackerEarth, Questionmark all gate pricing behind a sales conversation) and it exists because enterprise vendors need a human in the loop to handle custom/negotiated pricing. Pariksha has no such constraint — copying this pattern would manufacture friction and distrust for no reason, since "free and self-hosted" is a one-line, zero-ambiguity fact.
2. **Abstracting the headline away from the product category (iMocha's "The Context Layer for Skills & Work Intelligence").** A first-time visitor should know within one sentence that this is an online exam/assessment platform. Clever repositioning headlines work for Codility/CoderPad because their *subhead* immediately clarifies the category; don't copy the abstraction without also copying the immediate clarification.
3. **Engineer-coded brand voice bleeding into customer-facing surfaces (HackerRank's xkcd-comic 404 page).** Charming for a developer audience, wrong tone for a placement-cell director or training-institute owner — consistent with the brief's existing critique of Pariksha's own page (JSON snippets, "pre-alpha" everywhere). The target buyer for Pariksha is explicitly non-engineer.
4. **Proof-device sprawl without a hero story (Eklavvya's 9+ testimonials / 4+ case studies with no single standout).** More logos and more quotes is not more credible past a certain density; one well-told, numbers-backed case study (in Mercer Mettl's or Talview's style) beats a wall of logos.
5. **A "who this is NOT for" exclusion list as a trust device.** No site in this entire research set — not even the most engineer-coded ones (HackerRank, CoderPad before its 2026 repositioning) — uses a negative-framing exclusion list on the homepage. This appears to be unique to Pariksha's current page and has no precedent or evidence of working anywhere in the category; it should be cut rather than iterated on.
6. **A near-empty page with no proof devices at all when selling into a risk-averse, high-stakes buyer (ExamSoft's thin page, Testmoz's proof-free page).** Testmoz can get away with zero proof because its buyer (an individual teacher) has near-zero switching risk. Pariksha's buyer (a placement cell choosing the exam platform an entire graduating class will sit through once) has high stakes and needs at least the Mercer-Mettl/Talview level of named-proof density, not the Testmoz level.
7. **Leading with "pre-alpha" / version-maturity caveats in marketing copy.** Every mature-feeling page in this set (even scrappy ones like ClassMarker, est. 2006, still functional-looking) leads with capability and scale, and pushes any "early access"-type caveat (if present at all) to secondary/fine-print placement, never the hero or a dedicated list.
