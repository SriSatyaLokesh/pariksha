Why not a form

A form shows everyone the same paper.

Pariksha draws each candidate's paper from a pool you tag and filter, or hands out fixed sets A, B and C round-robin by registration order, so neighbours never sit the same questions. Options shuffle underneath, except the ones you pin.

  • The key never leaves the server. Not in the page, not in the payload.
  • The clock is the server's. Changing the computer's time does nothing; answers 30 seconds late are refused.
  • One roll number, one attempt. Any registration field can be marked unique.
  • Results you can act on. One CSV row per candidate, a column per section and per form field.

Integrity

No video. No audio. No verdicts.

Strict proctoring takes a 320×240 snapshot about every sixty seconds, counts faces on the device itself, and stores no biometric template. Nothing is streamed. Nothing is recorded. Snapshots sit in your own private bucket behind links that expire in ten minutes.

Tab switches, fullscreen exits and copy attempts are logged, and the candidate sees a counted warning rather than a silent mark against them. At the end Pariksha marks the attempt clean, review or high risk — and stops.

Browser checks deter and record. They cannot prove: a second phone is invisible to every one of them, and our own documentation says so. That is precisely why no machine here reaches a verdict.

What it costs

Five hundred candidates in an hour, for nothing.

By arithmetic rather than by a bill. The reference deployment is designed to fit inside Cloudflare's free tier — and there is no Pariksha billing of any kind, because there is no hosted service to bill for.

$0per candidate, forever. Your only cost is infrastructure you already own.
55kWorker requests for one test of 500, against a free limit of 100,000 a day.
0.75 GBof snapshots, against a free limit of 10 GB a month.
30 mintarget from clone to a first live test, with no paid service.

An estimate with published arithmetic. The 500-candidate figure is a design target; the load test that would prove it (AC-29) is a Phase 12 gate and has not been run. Free-tier limits can change — the mitigation is three adapter seams, so moving to Docker and Postgres is configuration, not a rewrite.

Where it stands

What exists today, and what lands with v0.1.

  • The question and test formats, and their JSON Schemas
  • All 12 question types, graded and unit-tested
  • All 5 scoring modes, enforced per type
  • Pool draws, set assignment, seeded shuffling
  • Cross-field import validation — one bad question imports nothing
  • A 17-question worked example covering all 12 types
  • The authoring UI and the test builder
  • The candidate runtime
  • Proctoring — Off, Standard and Strict
  • Results — CSV, live monitor, grading queue, item analysis
  • The API and signed webhooks
  • Organiser sign-in, roles and workspaces

A paid platform you can buy today will do more than Pariksha does today, because the application is not built yet. What is built is the format, the schemas and the scoring engine — and you can run those today.

What you can do today

  1. Write your question bank. The format is published and stable; questions written today import on day one — or into anything else, since the schemas are CC BY 4.0.
  2. Validate it in one command. One npx ajv run. No install, no account.
  3. Read a whole exam. Seventeen questions, three sections, the candidate's screen and the results CSV it produces.
