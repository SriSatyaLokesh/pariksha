SEC 03 / 11

Three hundred candidates, one week, and no budget.

A placement cell screening a graduating class. A training institute certifying a cohort. A sixty-person company that needs a shortlist by Friday. All three need the same four things: a different paper for every candidate, a clock nobody can edit, some idea of who was looking at another window, and a spreadsheet at the end.

A form tool gives you none of them. An assessment platform gives you all four and charges by the candidate, on its own servers, under its own terms. Pariksha is the third option: the same four things, running in an account you already have, for nothing.

SEC 04 / 11

A form shows everyone the same paper. An exam should not.

Pariksha draws each candidate's paper from a pool you tag and filter — four questions tagged quantitative, medium difficulty — or hands out fixed sets A, B and C round-robin by registration order, so neighbours never sit the same questions. Options shuffle underneath, except the ones you pin.

The answer key never leaves the server. The clock is the server's, so changing the computer's time does nothing, and an answer arriving thirty seconds after the deadline is refused. A field marked unique refuses a second registration with the same roll number.

SEC 05 / 11

Integrity without surveillance: no video, no audio, no verdicts.

Strict proctoring takes a 320×240 snapshot about every sixty seconds, counts faces on the device itself, and stores no biometric template. Nothing is streamed. Nothing is recorded. Snapshots sit in your own private bucket behind links that expire in ten minutes.

Tab switches, fullscreen exits and copy attempts are logged, and the candidate sees a counted warning rather than a silent mark against them. At the end Pariksha marks the attempt clean, review or high risk — and stops. A person decides what that means.

Browser checks deter and record. They cannot prove: a second phone is invisible to every one of them, and our own documentation says so. That is precisely why no machine here is allowed to reach a verdict.
