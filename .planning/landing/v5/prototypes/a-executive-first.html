Band 3 · The problem — name the situation in the buyer's words · 110 words

Three hundred candidates, one week, and no budget.

A placement cell screening a graduating class. A training institute certifying a cohort. A sixty-person company that needs a shortlist by Friday. All three need the same four things: a different paper for every candidate, a clock nobody can edit, some idea of who was looking at another window, and a spreadsheet at the end.

A form tool gives you none of them. An assessment platform gives you all four and charges by the candidate, on its own servers, under its own terms. Pariksha is the third option: the same four things, running in an account you already have, for nothing.

Band 4 · Pillar 1 — defeat the status quo (the form tool) · 180 words

A form shows everyone the same paper. An exam should not.

Pariksha draws each candidate's paper from a pool you tag and filter — four questions tagged quantitative, medium difficulty — or hands out fixed sets A, B and C round-robin by registration order, so neighbours never sit the same questions. Options shuffle underneath, except the ones you pin.

The answer key never leaves the server. The clock is the server's, so changing the computer's time does nothing, and an answer arriving thirty seconds after the deadline is refused. A field marked unique refuses a second registration with the same roll number.

Band 5 · What you get — capability inventory, tagged per feature · 220 words

Twelve question types, five scoring modes, one open file.

Write questions worth asking live

Twelve types including matching, multi-matching, ordering and numeric with tolerance. Markdown, LaTeX, code blocks and images in every one.

Score them your way live

All-or-nothing, partial credit, negative marking, position-based ordering, rubrics. Change a key afterwards and regrade every attempt, with an audit entry.

Give everyone a different paper live

Pools filtered by tag and difficulty, fixed sets A/B/C, and seeded shuffling that survives a refresh.

Build the test v0.1

A type picker, one editing screen for all twelve types, drag-to-reorder sections, live pool counts and a publish checklist.

Run it and watch it v0.1

A registration form you design, access codes, a server clock, a live monitor, and three proctoring profiles.

Get the results v0.1

One CSV row per candidate: a column per section and per registration field, rank, pass/fail and the integrity flag.

You never write JSON.

The format exists so that you can — and so that nothing you build is trapped.

{
  "id": "LOG-001",
  "type": "single",
  "prompt": "What comes next in the series?\n\n3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ?",
  "key": "c",
  "scoring": { "mode": "all_or_nothing", "points": 2 },
  "explanation": "Each term is double the previous term plus one: 63 × 2 + 1 = 127."
}

The key and the explanation live in the file, never in the candidate's browser.

Band 6 · Pillar 3 — the project's most defensible position · 170 words

Integrity without surveillance: no video, no audio, no verdicts.

Strict proctoring takes a 320×240 snapshot about every sixty seconds, counts faces on the device itself, and stores no biometric template. Nothing is streamed. Nothing is recorded. Snapshots sit in your own private bucket behind links that expire in ten minutes, and the default is to delete them after ninety days.

Tab switches, fullscreen exits and copy attempts are logged, and the candidate sees a counted warning rather than a silent mark against them. At the end Pariksha marks the attempt clean, review or high risk — and stops. A person decides what that means.

Browser checks deter and record. They cannot prove: a second phone is invisible to every one of them, and our own documentation says so. That is precisely why no machine here is allowed to reach a verdict.

Band 7 · Privacy and control — the procurement answer, before it is asked · 150 words

Your account, your database, your retention rules.

Candidate data lives in your own Cloudflare account or your own server. There is no Pariksha server in the path, no telemetry, and no third-party script anywhere — including on this page, which serves its own fonts under a default-src 'self' policy.

You choose the database region and the storage jurisdiction where your host allows it. Retention defaults to ninety days for snapshots and twelve months for personal data and answers, and a daily job deletes and logs it. v0.1 A candidate can ask for their data to be exported or erased. Every sign-in, role change, publish, key change, regrade, export and deletion lands in an append-only audit log. v0.1

The consent screen is text you write. A data-processing template for GDPR and India's DPDP Act 2023 ships with the documentation.

Band 8 · Cost — remove the price objection with evidence · 160 words

Five hundred candidates in an hour, for nothing. Here is the arithmetic.

ResourceFree limitOne test of 500
Worker requests100,000 / dayabout 55,000
Database rows written100,000 / dayabout 55,000
Object storage10 GB / monthabout 0.75 GB

An estimate with published arithmetic. The 500-candidate figure is a design target; the load test that would prove it (AC-29) is a Phase 12 gate and has not been run.

The pricing model, in full: nothing, ever, to Pariksha. There is no Pariksha billing of any kind and no hosted service to bill for. Your only cost is infrastructure you already own — or the free tier you already have.

Free-tier limits can change. That is the named risk against the zero-cost estimate, and the mitigation is the architecture: three adapter seams — database, object storage, identity — so moving to Docker and Postgres is configuration, not a rewrite.

Band 12 · Built in the open — licence, decisions, contribution · 140 words

Read the code, change it, or sell a service built on it.

Apache-2.0, with an explicit patent grant: self-host it, modify it, embed it, or offer a paid service built on it — including a closed one. Documentation, schemas and example tests are CC BY 4.0. There is no contributor licence agreement.

Every architectural decision is a dated record you can disagree with — why Cloudflare with adapters, why Apache-2.0 — and the build order is twelve public phases.

The most useful contribution right now needs no code: add a question set in your own subject under examples/, translate interface strings, or write a real test in pariksha/test@1 and report what you could not express.

Band 13 · FAQ — nine existing answers kept verbatim, five added

Questions and answers

Who is Pariksha for?

Placement cells, training institutes, certification bodies and small companies that must test hundreds of people, have little or no budget for it, and need candidate data to stay in their own account.

What does it cost per candidate?

Nothing. There is no Pariksha billing and no hosted service to bill for. Your only cost is the infrastructure you already own or the free tier you already have.

Do I need a developer to run it?

Once, to create a Cloudflare account or run Docker. Everyday use — writing questions, building tests, reading results — needs no code at all.

What happens to my tests if the project stops?

They keep working. Apache-2.0 with a patent grant, and every test exports as one documented JSON file under CC BY 4.0. Nothing you build depends on this project continuing to exist.
